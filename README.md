## Play

At fighter select, **Try fighters** lets you play each dog’s signature move and offstage recovery before picking for the highlighted seat. Use P1 controls throughout the tryout. In **Rooftop shuttle**, cross the gold post, reverse, and cross the mint start line on the roof. The clock starts on your first input. Each dog keeps its best time on this device, and a moving marker shows where the fastest saved dog was on the same frame. Choose **Tryout card** to switch dogs, then **Pick** to bring your choice back to the match you set up. [Try all four dogs](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/tryouts)

**Clover’s Disc Range** is on her fighter tryout card. Break the three plates from the chalk on the Picnic Table bench in six throws. Hold toward the plates and press Smash to start Line Drive. Keep forward held and add Up or Down during the windup to aim, then release Smash. More charge gives the disc enough range for the far plate. Watch the example for all three throws, or set a best using the fewest throws and then the fastest time. [Play Disc Range](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#arcade/disc-range)

Clover can pitch the same side smash in ordinary fights. Aim high to catch a short hop or send a low disc off the lip at a returning rival. A downward shot stops at the solid floor. Opponents can shield it or reflect it with Bark. Updated online peers share the new trajectories in rooms and quickplay.

After a launch costs you a stock, open **KO replays → Compare launch steering**. The dashed gold path shows the recorded flight. Pick a direction to see where the same hit takes your dog when you change the stick on the release frame. The solid mint path ends at a knockout or at the last recorded frame. **Play from impact** gives you control on that frame before release. After losing your first Fight School match by knockout, **Replay my last stock** opens the finish directly. To compare two clear outcomes, let Brisket throw Pip from the high platform, pause after the knockout, and compare **Center** with **Left**. [Play the top-platform opening](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/throw-read/perch)

Choose **Find a route back onstage** in that comparison to test launch steering and follow-ups from your own hit. When the simulation finds a landing, watch the whole return at half speed or try it yourself. **Guide my inputs** waits at the hit and again for the recovery input. To practice before losing a stock, open **Fight School → Launch survival**. In the high throw, steer Pip left at release, drift right, then air dodge down to survive the top blast line. **Try the edge throw** gives you a different hit to survive with Fence Hopper. [Play Launch survival](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#tutorial/launch-survival)

In **Adventure**, pause a mission and choose **Suspend & quit** to save the fight on this device. Home shows the mission name, stocks, and damage. Choose **Resume mission**, then **Resume fight** when you’re ready. Fighters, grabs, moving platforms, and boss attacks resume on the saved frame. Each mission has its own slot. Suspending again replaces that mission’s save, and clearing the resumed mission retires it. [Open Adventure](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#adventure)

In **Training → Find a drill → Chase the up throw**, tap Attack to grab and throw with the selected dog. You control the chase as soon as the throw releases. Try **Launch DI in** and **Launch DI out** to see both flight paths, then choose **Mix launch DI** and read the rival in the air. Land a follow-up before they touch down. A fresh Attack after the result starts another attempt. The same three rival responses work with your recorded combos. [Try Brisket against Pip at 60%](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#training/throw-chase)

In **KO replays** or **Watch finish**, open **Recorded inputs** to inspect the steering and buttons on the displayed frame. Use **Previous change** and **Next change** to find a shield release, a jump tap, or the direction of a strike. Solo records your controls. Local and online fights let you switch between P1 and P2, and online replays use confirmed inputs. [Try it after a last-stock fight](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/last-stock)

When a grab or hit leads to a knockout, open **Defend the grab** or **Defend the hit** in the replay. Choose how many frames before contact to start. Compare the recording with Shield, a spot-dodge, Jump or Attack. In midair, Shield becomes an air dodge. Watch each reply at half speed, then choose **Try it myself** to play from that exact frame. Solo opponents can react to the change. In local and online recordings, the other player repeats their inputs. On Night Mail, let Brisket throw Pip off the left edge, open **Pause → KO replays**, and compare holding Shield with tapping Attack before his Bear Hug. [Play the left-ledge opening](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/throw-read/edge)

Choose **Random dog** at fighter select to draw a dog for the highlighted player or CPU. The starting dog and the first three draws cover the whole roster. Between games of a set, the player who lost can draw a new challenger. [Pick together](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#local/pick-together)

A coin flip gives one player the first strike, the other removes two arenas, and the first removes one more. Game one takes place on the remaining arena. Between games the winner bans two stages and the loser picks from the other three. In local play the loser can also switch dogs and coats. Use your movement keys or D-pad to highlight a stage and Attack or A to confirm. On touch, choose an arena tile and confirm the named action. [Open Online versus](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#online)

In Quickplay and single-game rooms, when both players select **Rematch**, the arena advances through Picnic Table, Laundry Line, Moonbell Bandstand, Night Mail, and Canal Lock in that order, then repeats. If the guest leaves, the next opponent can join the same code and play the host’s chosen opener.

After Quickplay, a single room game, or a completed set, either player can choose **Change fighter**. Pick a dog and coat, then choose **Ready as** to lock in. **Back to results** discards the pick. **Rematch** keeps your current dog and coat. Your opponent sees when you are ready and discovers your pick as the next match starts. If both players choose the same coat on the same dog, P2 wears the next color.

[Set up an online best-of-three](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#online/best-of-three)

Your dog responds on the next simulation frame. Both browsers predict the opponent’s held controls for up to eight frames and replay the fight when a late input changes what happened. The damage counter, stocks, points, and match result use confirmed inputs. A longer interruption briefly pauses the fight until the connection catches up.

Online play uses PeerServer Cloud to find the other browser, then exchanges inputs over a direct WebRTC data channel. Both players need internet access and a network that permits the connection. The bundled PeerJS 1.5.5 client includes its MIT license in `docs/vendor/`. [PeerServer Cloud](https://peerjs.com/server/cloud)

## Modes and controls

- **Solo** pits your dog against a computer opponent.
- **Options** saves sound, master volume, music level and camera shake in this browser. Use the arrow keys, D-pad or left stick to move between the buttons you see. Moving back across the large Adventure cover returns to the same row. Hold a pad direction to scroll through choices. Left and right adjust a focused slider or select field. **A** confirms, **B** backs out, and **Start** launches the highlighted setup from fighter select. Returning from a setup screen restores focus to the mode that opened it. Keyboard **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** follow reading order.
- **Customize controls** in Options or Controls saves separate P1 and P2 layouts. Choose a keyboard action and press its new key, or choose a controller button from the list. The editor shows both assignments before a swap. **Test layout** lets you hold a chord and see exactly which actions register. Fight School and the controls guide use your saved inputs. Solo, Fight School and online play use P1. In local versus, a lone controller belongs to P2, and two controllers drive one dog each. [Customize controls](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#controls)
- **Sound Test** plays the menu theme, all five arena tunes and each dog's victory fanfare. In a stock fight, the arena band changes its arrangement on the next bar when both fighters reach their last stock. Try the ordinary and final-stock versions of *Clothesline Waltz* on Laundry Line, then compare it with *Overnight Freight* on Night Mail. The music level can go to zero while combat cues stay audible. [Open Sound Test](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#sound-test)
- **Local versus** lets two people play on one device. Select either nameplate in the arena preview, then choose that side's dog from the roster.
- **Training** starts you within striking distance of a stationary dummy. It shows the last move you used and counts hits that connect before the dummy can act again. Reset positions, change the dummy's starting damage, or practice getting back from the ledge.
- **Fetch Ball** adds a tennis ball that either dog can catch on the ground or in midair. Tap Grab as it reaches your paws. Carry it across a platform, then aim and tap Attack or Grab for a forward toss, a high lob, or a downward bounce. A hit or an opponent’s grab knocks it loose. Swatting a loose ball adds charge. The third swat turns it gold, and a catch keeps the stored charge. Try the three-stock opening with a two-paw ball at your feet. [Play solo Fetch](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/fetch) · [Play local Fetch](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#local/fetch)
- **Air drop and catch.** While airborne with the ball, let go of the direction and tap Grab. The ball falls from your paw and you can act immediately. Tap Attack while it’s still in reach to catch it as your aerial starts. Either dog can make the catch. A two-paw drop deals 4% and a gold drop deals 5%. Catch the gold ball and throw it for its full 19%. On Night Mail, try letting the ball land on your rival, then play the other side and hold Shield to see how the Rival answers. [Try the drop](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/drop-catch) · [Defend the landing](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#solo/defend-drop)

Flick a side to dash. Reverse in the first few steps to dash-dance. Once your dog is running, its paws have to brake before it can turn. Pip can reverse a dash later than Brisket, who skids farther when stopping. To walk on touch, slide from the center dot onto a side arrow. A direct tap on the arrow starts a dash.

Walk to the edge of a platform and your dog plants its paws at the lip. You can tilt, shield, jump, or turn back immediately. Dash to take the fight offstage. If you land an aerial near a platform tip with enough sideways speed, sliding off ends its remaining landing recovery. You keep an air jump and can swing on the way down.

In **Training → Edge practice**, walk to the chalk and catch a rival’s ledge jump with **Down + Attack**. Then compare **Land at the tip** and **Land deeper**. Tap neutral Attack and coast in both. In **Training → Footwork practice**, dash right to the chalk and reverse to draw out Brisket’s Bear Hug. As his paws close, turn back and attack before he recovers. The drill reports how many frames he still needed when your punish landed. Choose **Footwork practice** again to retry with the same spacing.

## Grounded shield defense

Hold **Shield** on the ground to block. On a touchscreen, keep one thumb on Shield and use the other on the direction pad.

- **Shield + Left or Right** rolls in that direction, even through a rival. A forward roll turns you around to face the dog you passed. A backward roll keeps your facing, travels a little less, and takes two extra frames to recover.
- **Shield + Down** spot-dodges in place. Time the duck to slip a grab and stay close enough to answer. Both dodges have a brief invincible stretch between their vulnerable start and recovery. Keep holding Shield after the dodge to block again.
- **Release Shield just before a strike** to parry. The first five frames of the release can catch the hit. A melee parry interrupts the attacker and gives you an opening to counter. A projectile parry absorbs the impact at your position. Grabs can catch a blocking dog or a dog trying to parry.
- A blocked jab gives a short pause and a small shove. A charged smash holds the shield longer and pushes the dogs farther apart. A blocked projectile can pin the defender while its owner keeps moving.
- A hard block can push your paws past a platform edge. Your shield drops as you slip, with one air jump available. At a solid ledge, you can let yourself fall into a catch. On a thin platform, you fall toward the floor below. Leaving room behind you or parrying the blow can keep you onstage.

- **Jump**, **Grab**, **Up + Smash**, and **Up + Special** work directly from a grounded shield. Keep Shield held and tap a response during impact or shieldstun. It starts on the first actionable frame. An immediate shield grab adds four frames to the dog's usual grab startup. Holding Up + Smash charges it as usual.

## Air dodges

Tap **Shield** in midair with the stick centered or the direction keys released for a **neutral air dodge**. Your dog tucks in place, lets the hit pass, then starts falling. Aim as you tap Shield for a **directional air dodge**. You can burst left, right, up, down, or diagonally. On a controller the left stick sets the angle. On touch, hold an arrow and tap Shield with your other thumb. Lift your direction thumb or slide it to the center dot for neutral.

A directional dodge commits you to its original angle. From outside the stage, aim diagonally up and inward to slip a guard and grab the ledge. Both dodges have vulnerable startup and recovery. The directional dodge travels farther and takes longer to recover. You get one air dodge until you land or catch a ledge. A midair jump and Up + Special remain available afterward if you saved them.

## Ledge trumping

Catch the same lip as a hanging dog to take their place. A falling catch, a directional air dodge and Noodle’s Ledge Lasso can all do it. The displaced dog pops outward without taking damage and can jump, attack, or use a recovery special after the brief recoil. Rolling, jumping or dropping before the other dog arrives leaves the lip free.

A short first catch at 0% protects you for 15 frames. A longer flight adds protected time and higher damage takes it away. A fresh catch lasts between 6 and 45 frames. Watch the grip marks beneath the lip. The final mark turns gold for the last six frames, then disappears when an opponent can hit or grab you. Jump or drop early to carry up to 14 remaining frames into your exit. Releasing and catching either ledge again gives zero protection until you land or take a hit. You can still trump someone with a vulnerable regrab.

In **Edge practice**, compare **Short recovery** and **Long recovery**. Tap Attack to start the rival’s approach. Time a low tilt as the grip marks disappear, before the rival rolls. Change **Dummy damage** and repeat the same input to see which hit connects. [Play the ledge catch drill](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#training/ledge-catch).

Open **Training → Contested ledge**. **Take the ledge** starts you outside a rival’s grip. Drift inward or use your recovery. **Defend the ledge** starts you hanging while the rival approaches. Wait for the bump, tap Shield to roll clear, or drop early and try a back air. Select Noodle as the dummy to practice against Ledge Lasso. **Switch ledge** mirrors both roles.

**Run-off back air.** Dash off the occupied ledge and steer inward as you fall. When your dog takes the ledge, press Down + Jump together. Release Down, aim away from the stage and tap Attack for your back aerial. Steer inward during the swing to land. Guided practice pauses for the turn, the drop jump and the back air. Turn it off to perform the sequence at match speed. [Practice the follow-up](https://caleb-guyer.github.io/dog-fight-3/#training/runoff-trump)

## Source and artwork

The editable game lives in `docs/`. Combat, physics, and the CPU are in `engine.js`. The match renderer is in `render.js`. The animated dogs and arenas are drawn in `fighters-art.js` and `arena-art.js`. Menus, controls, and synthesized sound are in `app.js`.

Each fighter is assembled from indexed pixel sprites. The roster, damage meter, and results screen take their portraits from that fighter's in-game artwork. The arenas are drawn on a 400 × 225 pixel grid. The contact sparks, landing dust, and launch trails share a five-frame atlas in `docs/assets/fight-fx.png`. The Dog Fight 3 wordmark and Brawl Pixel display font are bundled with the game.
